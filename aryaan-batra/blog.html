Market check 17 August 2026

Auckland winter check-up: more choice, patient buyers, honest prices winning

The July REINZ numbers are out, and they tell a consistent story: this is a buyer's-choice market where realistically priced homes are the ones selling. Auckland's median sale price came in at $940,000, down 3.6% on a year ago, while new Auckland listings actually rose 9.4% year on year. Nationally, unsold inventory has reached a 12-year high of just over 33,000 homes, and the typical property is taking around 50 days to sell.

Auctions are running at winter levels too. In the week to 14 August, 38% of monitored auctions nationally sold under the hammer, the first time in a month the rate has dipped below 40%, and auction volumes earlier in August were the lowest since 2023. That's the season, not a verdict: auction rooms consistently pick up with spring listings.

Here's the part that matters if you're selling in South Auckland: property analysts Cotality report that sellers who held their homes long term are still banking a median gain of $280,000 on resale, while short-hold resales (typically around four years) are where losses concentrate. And realestate.co.nz reports the sellers winning right now are the ones "priced to the current market conditions". Translation: price honestly on day one, meet the market where it is, and your home stands out against 33,000 alternatives.

  • Auckland median: $940,000 (July, down 3.6% YoY) — REINZ
  • National inventory: 33,252 homes, a 12-year high — realestate.co.nz
  • Auction clearance: 38% nationally, week to 14 August — interest.co.nz
  • Median hold time behind resale gains: 10+ years — Cotality

Sources: REINZ July 2026 data · interest.co.nz auction monitoring · Cotality resale analysis

Interest rates 17 August 2026

The OCR just turned: what July's rate hike means for your next move

On 8 July the Reserve Bank raised the Official Cash Rate to 2.50%, its first hike in three years, to steer inflation back to 2%. Several major lenders have already lifted fixed mortgage rates in response, with one-year rates sitting in the mid-4% range and rising. Most major-bank economists now expect the OCR to reach around 3.00% by the end of 2026, and the next review lands on 2 September.

What does that mean practically? If you're buying, the cheap-money window that reopened in 2025 is narrowing: every 25 basis points adds real dollars to fortnightly repayments, so a pre-approval locked in now is worth more than one shopped for at Christmas. If you're selling, this is worth taking seriously as a timing signal. Buyer budgets are set by what banks will lend, and rising rates gradually trim those budgets. A campaign run this spring meets buyers whose approvals were priced at today's rates, not next year's.

One more wrinkle: REINZ reports investors sitting on their hands ahead of November's election while first-home buyers stay unusually active. In South Auckland's price brackets, that first-home demand is exactly who turns up at open homes, and they're motivated to beat further rate rises.

Sources: OCR announcement, July 2026 · interest.co.nz economist survey · REINZ July commentary

South vs Central 17 August 2026

Two markets, one city: first-home energy in the south, value hunting in the centre

South Auckland is being carried by first-home buyers, and it's not a small effect. Nationally, first-home buyers took a record 28.3% share of purchases in the June quarter, and in Auckland their share has been running around 30%. That demand lands squarely on South Auckland's heartland stock: the townhouses and family homes of Papatoetoe, Manurewa, Clover Park and the Heights suburbs. Meanwhile the growth corridor keeps growing — Flat Bush has passed 45,000 residents, nearly double its 2018 population. The honest flip side: the $800,000 to $1.4 million bracket is competitive for sellers right now, with plenty of stock and auction rooms selective, so presentation and pricing do the heavy lifting.

Central Auckland is a value hunter's market. CBD apartments have been transacting well below council valuations (a Ray White Auckland Central analysis earlier this year put the median gap around 14%), and with the City Rail Link opening, international evidence collected by CBRE suggests properties within a short walk of the new stations can command premiums of 5% to 15% over time. For a South Auckland owner thinking about a first investment, that combination — discounted entry prices next to brand-new infrastructure — is worth understanding properly.

Rents tell a steady story: Barfoot & Thompson's Auckland average weekly rent sits at $699 (up 0.9% on a year ago) while Trade Me's median asking rent is $660 (down 1.5%). The two measure different things, managed-portfolio averages versus asking prices, but together they say the same thing: flat-to-gently-firm, with well-presented, warm homes letting fastest.
