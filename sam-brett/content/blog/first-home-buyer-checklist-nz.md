# First-Home Buyer Guide NZ: 7 Steps and a Checklist Before You Buy

**Buying your first home in New Zealand comes down to seven steps: work out your budget, save your deposit (including any KiwiSaver you can withdraw), get mortgage pre-approval, decide what you're looking for, attend open homes, complete your due diligence, and understand how the property is being sold before you make an offer.** This guide walks through each step and finishes with a checklist you can use before you buy.

Buying your first home is an exciting milestone, but knowing where to start can feel overwhelming. Here's what you need to know before you take your first step onto the property ladder.

## Step 1: Work out your budget

Before you look at properties, understand what you can afford. Consider your income, savings, existing debts and ongoing living expenses. Then factor in the extra costs of buying and owning a home:

- Legal (conveyancing) fees
- Building inspection and LIM report
- House insurance
- Council rates
- Ongoing maintenance

A mortgage adviser or your bank can help you understand your borrowing capacity and set a realistic budget.

## Step 2: Save your deposit

Your deposit is one of the biggest steps towards buying your first home. **In New Zealand, a 20% deposit is the common benchmark, but some lenders offer first-home buyer lending with a smaller deposit, subject to eligibility and lending criteria.**

### Can I use KiwiSaver for my first home?

You may be eligible to use your KiwiSaver savings towards your first home if you meet the withdrawal requirements, including at least three years of membership. Check the current rules with your KiwiSaver provider.

## Step 3: Get mortgage pre-approval

Mortgage pre-approval tells you how much a lender is likely to lend you before you start seriously looking. It helps you:

- Establish your price range
- Understand your likely repayments
- Make more informed property decisions
- Move quickly and confidently when you make an offer

Pre-approval is conditional and isn't a guarantee of final finance approval.

## Step 4: Decide what you're looking for

Think about the type of property and location that suits your lifestyle. Consider:

- Location and distance to work
- Number of bedrooms and bathrooms
- Parking and outdoor space
- Nearby schools, shops and amenities
- Public transport and local facilities

Make a list of must-haves and nice-to-haves to narrow your search. If you're considering South Auckland, our [South Auckland first home buyer suburb guide](/blog/first-home-buyer-guide-south-auckland/) compares Wattle Downs, Manurewa, Hill Park, Totara Park and The Gardens.

## Step 5: Attend open homes

Open homes are the best way to compare properties and learn what you really want. When viewing, look closely at the home's overall condition: signs of dampness or mould, maintenance issues, natural light, ventilation and how the space works. Ask questions, and visit several open homes before making a decision.

## Step 6: Do your due diligence

Before committing to a property, do your research. Depending on the property, this may include:

- Organising a building inspection
- Reviewing the LIM (Land Information Memorandum) report
- Checking the property title
- Understanding any council restrictions or unconsented work
- Reviewing body corporate information for units and townhouses
- Confirming you can get insurance

Always have your lawyer review the sale and purchase agreement before you sign.

## Step 7: Understand how the property is being sold

In New Zealand, homes are usually sold by **auction, deadline sale (tender), price by negotiation or a set asking price**. Each method works differently. Make sure you understand the process before you make an offer. We're happy to explain how each method works, arrange viewings and communicate with the seller on your behalf.

Once your offer is accepted and all conditions are met, you'll move towards **settlement day**, when ownership transfers to you and you get the keys to your new home.

## Your first-home buyer checklist

Before you buy, make sure you've:

- Worked out your budget, including buying costs
- Saved your deposit
- Checked your KiwiSaver first-home withdrawal eligibility
- Got mortgage pre-approval
- Researched locations and properties
- Attended open homes
- Completed your due diligence (building report, LIM, title)
- Had your lawyer review the sale and purchase agreement
- Arranged finance and insurance

## Frequently asked questions for first-home buyers

### How much deposit do I need for my first home in NZ?
In New Zealand, a 20% deposit is a common benchmark, although some lenders may offer options with a smaller deposit, subject to eligibility and lending criteria. A mortgage adviser can tell you what's available to you.

### How long does it take to buy a house in NZ?
It varies. Once your offer is accepted, you'll need to satisfy any conditions (such as finance and a building inspection) before moving to settlement day, when ownership transfers and you get the keys. Your lawyer and agent can talk you through the expected timeframes for your purchase.

### What costs do first-home buyers forget about?
Common extra costs include legal fees, building inspections, LIM reports, insurance, council rates, moving costs and ongoing maintenance. Build these into your budget from the start.

### Do I need a lawyer to buy a house in NZ?
Yes. A lawyer or licensed conveyancer handles the legal transfer of the property, and you should have them review the sale and purchase agreement before you sign.
