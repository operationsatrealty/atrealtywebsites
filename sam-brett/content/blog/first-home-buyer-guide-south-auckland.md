# First Home Buyer Guide: South Auckland Edition

Buying your first home is exciting—and it can feel overwhelming. This guide will walk you through the process and help you make confident decisions in the South Auckland market.

## Getting Started: Before You Even Look

### 1. Get Pre-Approved for a Mortgage

Before you start property hunting, talk to your bank or mortgage broker. Pre-approval shows:
- How much you can borrow
- What your monthly repayments will look like
- How serious you are to sellers

**South Auckland advantage**: Properties here offer excellent value compared to central Auckland, so your deposit often goes further.

### 2. Save a Deposit

Lenders typically want:
- **5–10%** deposit (first-home buyer schemes may lower this)
- First Home: Up to $650k with a 5% deposit (check current eligibility)
- Kiwisaver: You can withdraw funds for a first home

### 3. Budget for Additional Costs

Don't forget:
- Lawyer fees (~$1,200–$1,500)
- Building inspection (~$500–$700)
- Rates and council valuations
- Insurance

## Choosing Your Suburb

### Wattle Downs
- **Best for**: Families, investors
- **Median price**: $900k
- **Vibe**: Family-friendly, parks, schools
- **Commute**: 25–35 mins to CBD

### Hill Park
- **Best for**: Growing families, developers
- **Median price**: $850k
- **Vibe**: Newer subdivisions, new builds
- **Commute**: 30–40 mins to CBD

### Manurewa
- **Best for**: Buyers seeking established areas
- **Median price**: $920k
- **Vibe**: Well-established, schools, shops
- **Commute**: 25–30 mins to CBD

### Totara Park & The Gardens
- **Best for**: Quiet living, retirees, professionals
- **Median price**: $880k–$950k
- **Vibe**: Peaceful, low-density, green spaces
- **Commute**: 35–45 mins to CBD

## The Buying Process

### Step 1: Find a Property
Work with a buyer's agent or search online. Sam and Brett can guide you through available listings and what represents good value.

### Step 2: Make an Offer
Your agent or lawyer will help with this. In South Auckland, most sales go through negotiation rather than auction.

### Step 3: Building Inspection
**Don't skip this.** A good inspector will catch potential issues and give you negotiating power.

### Step 4: Lawyer & Settlement
Your lawyer handles the legal paperwork. This typically takes 4–6 weeks.

### Step 5: Move In
Settlement day is yours. Keys, new home, excitement—the payoff.

## Money-Saving Tips

1. **Buy off-peak** (winter slower months often mean better deals)
2. **Look beyond the obvious** (smaller sections, older homes can offer value)
3. **Consider renovation potential** (if you're handy or patient)
4. **Negotiate harder** (sellers often expect it—don't accept the first offer)
